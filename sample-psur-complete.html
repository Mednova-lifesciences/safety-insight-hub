The search returned 46 records, of which 5 were relevant on full-text review. Each is summarised below in the company's own words; no abstract has been reproduced.

  • A Nigerian retrospective cohort of 1,240 outpatients reported diarrhoea in 9.1% of those receiving the combination, a rate consistent with the frequency already stated in the RSI. The authors identified no unexpected reaction.
  • A European pharmacovigilance database analysis examined cholestatic hepatitis and found the reporting rate unchanged over ten years, with risk concentrated in courses exceeding 14 days — a duration outside the approved posology.
  • A paediatric tolerability review of the oral suspension in 318 children found no reaction not already described in the RSI, and no age-related difference in tolerability.
  • A case report described a single episode of acute generalised exanthematous pustulosis following a seven-day course. The reaction is listed in the RSI and the authors proposed no change to prescribing.

Appendix I — Reference Safety Information (SmPC), RSI version 6.2, 14 January 2026

Attached in full. Reproduced here in abbreviated form for the purposes of this synthetic test document. RMP version 4.1 (approved 09 October 2025) is referenced in Section 4.

1. Name of the medicinal product

Amoxicillin/Clavulanate Potassium 500 mg/125 mg film-coated tablets.

2. Qualitative and quantitative composition

Each film-coated tablet contains amoxicillin trihydrate equivalent to 500 mg amoxicillin and potassium clavulanate equivalent to 125 mg clavulanic acid.

4.1 Therapeutic indications

Treatment of bacterial infections of the upper and lower respiratory tract, urinary tract, skin and soft tissue, and odontogenic infections, where the organism is known or suspected to produce beta-lactamase.

4.2 Posology and method of administration

One tablet every eight hours. The usual course is 5 to 7 days and should not exceed 14 days without review.

4.3 Contraindications

Hypersensitivity to penicillins or to any excipient. Previous history of jaundice or hepatic dysfunction associated with amoxicillin/clavulanate.

4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use

Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity reactions have been reported with penicillins. Cholestatic jaundice and hepatitis have been reported, more frequently with prolonged treatment; treatment duration should not exceed 14 days without review. Antibiotic-associated colitis, including Clostridium difficile-associated diarrhoea, has been reported and should be considered in patients presenting with diarrhoea during or after treatment.
